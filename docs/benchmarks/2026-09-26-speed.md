# ThumbFree speed and accuracy, September 2026

All numbers come from a Pixel 10 (Tensor G5, Android 16) unless noted. "Stop to text" is the time from the tap that ends a take to the text appearing in the field, which is what a user feels.

## Stop tap to text, by length of speech

Measured on 26 September 2026 with that day's ThumbFree build plus a debug timing log, with airplane mode on so everything ran offline.

| Audio length | Stop tap to text |
|---|---|
| 3.7 s | 0.69 s |
| 7.5 s | 0.77 s |
| 11.0 s (JFK) | 0.88 s |
| 15.1 s | 0.76 s |
| 18.3 s | 0.88 s |
| 29.4 s | 0.90 s |

Medians of 3 runs per clip. Word error rate over the same runs: 7.4%, with the audio played through a speaker (see the caveats).

The time stays under a second at every length because ThumbFree transcribes a long take in pieces while you are still talking, so only the last few seconds are left when you stop.

**Method.** The clips are public LibriSpeech recordings plus JFK, played from the Mac's speaker next to the phone, dictated into ThumbFree's own practice box. A debug build logs each change of that box (its length and the phone's clock in milliseconds), and the stop tap's time is read on the phone right after the tap lands. Nothing on the screen was recorded. The practice box and its log were later removed from the app (the Home tab replaced the Try tab), so this method can't be repeated as written.

**Caveats.**
- 3 runs per length is a small sample.
- The speaker-to-microphone path adds recognition errors that a real voice close to the phone does not have.
- The first run after setup was slower (1.7 s) while the engine warmed up; medians are used.

## What made ThumbFree fast

| Change | Effect on the Pixel 10 |
|---|---|
| Engine patches 0001 to 0003: Q8_0 weights in place, CPU repack with i8mm | JFK stop to text 2.27 s to 1.37 s |
| Audio front end (mel filterbank) fused into the FFT pass | 85 ms to 11 ms on an 11 s take |
| One long-lived thread pool for the speech engine | decoder 64 ms to 15 ms on JFK |
| Engine threads on the fast cores, with Android performance hints (ADPF) | engine time 406 to 201 ms (3.7 s take), 675 to 480 ms (JFK); energy per take down 27% on short takes |
| The stop tail ends once the speaker is clearly done, with silence padded back in | median tail 360 ms to 200 ms (140 ms in a quiet room), no accuracy loss |

A 10-minute back-to-back test stayed at thermal status 0. Its throughput settled about 25% below the first minute, which is still about 40% ahead of the old settings in every minute. If the phone does heat up, ThumbFree steps down to fewer cores between takes.

## Parakeet vs Canary (why Parakeet is the default)

| Measure | Parakeet Unified EN 0.6B | Canary 180M Flash |
|---|---|---|
| Stop to text, 11 s take | 1.41 s | 2.78 s |
| Stop to text, 3.7 s take | 0.61 s | 1.49 s |
| CPU time per minute of audio | 16.7 s | 51.3 s |
| Energy per minute of audio | 15.4 J | 27.4 J |
| Word error rate (11 public clips) | 3.47% | 3.47% |
| Engine memory at rest | 659 MB | 293 MB |

Measured before the thread and front-end work above, with both models on the same engine build. Canary stays available as an experimental option.

## Multilingual model

The multilingual model (Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3, 25 European languages) runs as fast as the English one. Measured on the Pixel 10 on 26 September 2026, engine only: the app's call into its speech engine for the whole clip, 3 rounds of 10 calls per clip and model, the two models alternating.

| Audio length | English model (Parakeet Unified EN 0.6B) | Multilingual model (Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3) |
|---|---|---|
| 3.7 s | 227 ms | 222 ms |
| 8.4 s (German) | 414 ms | 427 ms |
| 11.0 s (JFK) | 546 ms | 568 ms |
| 29.4 s | 1,446 ms | 1,459 ms |

Medians of 30 calls. The rest of a take runs the same code with either model, so their stop to text differs only by the difference in this call, at most 22 ms here.

## Live preview

The live preview (Settings > Bubble > Show words while I speak) shows your words by the bubble while you talk; the text typed at the stop is the same either way.

Measured on the Pixel 10, engine level, 3 rounds of 30 s and 60 s takes, preview off against on (27 September 2026, with that day's build). Each take plays JFK at real time into the app's speech engine the way the app records, in a shuffled order, each starting at thermal status 0.

| Take | Preview's real-time factor | Stop to text | Engine peak memory | Thermal status | Energy per minute |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 s, preview off | | 402 ms | 912 MB | 0 | 42 J |
| 30 s, preview on | 0.261 | 492 ms | 912 MB | 0 | 127 J |
| 60 s, preview off | | 833 ms | 914 MB | 0 | 45 J |
| 60 s, preview on | 0.273 | 862 ms | 913 MB | 0 | 131 J |

Medians of 3 takes; memory and thermal status are the highest of the 3. Stop to text here is the engine's part only, from the end of the audio to the last piece's text.

The rules for turning the preview on by default:

| Rule | Result |
|---|---|
| It keeps up: never behind, median real-time factor at most 0.35 | Pass (0.261 and 0.273) |
| The same typed text with and without it | Pass (6 of 6 pairs) |
| Stop to text at most 100 ms slower | Pass (+90 ms and +29 ms) |
| Energy per minute at most 2 times | **Fail (3.03 and 2.92 times)** |
| Thermal status at most 1 | Pass (0) |
| Engine peak memory under 1.2 GB | Pass (914 MB) |

Every rule passed except energy, at about 3 times, so the preview is off. It is an experimental feature: the code and its tests stay, and the app doesn't offer it. It costs battery only while you dictate: it runs only while a take records.
