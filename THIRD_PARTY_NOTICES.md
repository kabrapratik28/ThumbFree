### wordfreq word list (share-alike)

`CommonWords.kt` (Android) and `CommonWords.swift` (iOS, the same list) hold the 5,000 most frequent English words from
wordfreq 3.1.1 by Robyn Speer, turned into lowercase keys. wordfreq's data is licensed under
[CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/), and so are these two files; the rest of ThumbFree's
code is Apache 2.0. If you change the list and share it, share your version
under CC BY-SA 4.0 too.

wordfreq's data comes from Google Books Ngrams, the Leeds Internet Corpus (University of Leeds Centre for Translation
Studies), Wikipedia, ParaCrawl, OPUS OpenSubtitles 2018 (from the OpenSubtitles project), and the SUBTLEX word lists
by Marc Brysbaert et al. SUBTLEX is freely available data. Citation: Robyn Speer (2022), rspeer/wordfreq: v3.0,
Zenodo, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7199437.

### Unicode and CLDR data (the iOS emoji picker)

`ios/tools/gen-emoji.py` reads [Unicode's emoji-test.txt](https://www.unicode.org/Public/emoji/) and the English
annotations of [CLDR](https://github.com/unicode-org/cldr-json), each pinned to a release, and writes the names,
versions, skin tones and keywords the picker uses into `ios/Shared/EmojiData.swift`. The emoji themselves are drawn by
iOS: ThumbFree ships no emoji images. The picker's order, and the keyboard's layouts and long-press alternatives, are
facts read from Apple's keyboard on a Simulator (`ios/tools/emoji/`, `ios/tools/keyboard/`); no Apple file is copied.

## Reference code, not included

[FluidAudio](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio) (Apache 2.0) is the reference runtime for the iOS app's Core
ML models. The iOS engine tests compare ThumbFree's text with its output, and
`ios/ThumbFreeKit/Sources/TFEngine/TdtDecoder.swift` follows its model calls in ThumbFree's own code. None of its code
is copied or linked.
